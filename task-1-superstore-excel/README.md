For this task I used the Superstore sales dataset, which has about 10,000 order lines from 2014 to 2017, and built a sales dashboard in Excel. I used pivot tables for total revenue, units sold, sales by region, sales and profit by category, monthly sales, and profit by discount level, and put the charts together on one dashboard sheet with a region slicer. I also added a month-over-month change column on a separate sheet, and helper columns for year, month and profit margin.

Total sales come to 2,297,200.86, with 37,873 units sold and 286,397.02 in profit. West and East bring in about 61% of the revenue. Sales go up from 2014 to 2017 and are strongest between September and December. The most interesting thing I found is that furniture sells about as much as the other categories but only makes about 2.5% profit, while office supplies and technology make about 17%. Tables and bookcases are the ones losing money. I also found that every discount level of 30% or more loses money. That's about 14% of the order lines and around 135,000 in lost profit. This shows the two move together, and I can't say from this data that the discounts caused the loss.

One problem I had was with the dates. The file stores them as month/day/year, but my Excel read them the other way round, so I re-imported the file with the right date format before building anything. I checked every pivot table against the totals (2,297,200.86 in sales and 286,397.02 in profit) to make sure nothing was lost or counted twice.
